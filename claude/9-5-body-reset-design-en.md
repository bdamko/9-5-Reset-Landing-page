# Design: 9–5 Body Reset

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-06-08
Branch: main
Status: DRAFT
Mode: Startup

---

## Problem Statement

Office workers with dense calendars have no system for body recovery during the day.
The barrier isn't motivation — it's decision cost. To act on a gap between meetings,
a person must: (1) notice the gap exists, (2) calculate how much time they have,
(3) decide what to do with it, (4) motivate themselves to start. Most people fail
at step 1 or 3. The app removes all four steps and hands the user a ready action.

The calendar is the unlock. Not "time to stretch!" but "you have 11 minutes before
your 2pm — quick desk reset ready." That specificity is what makes it actionable.

---

## Demand Evidence

None validated externally yet. Founder is the target user — the problem is lived,
not hypothesized. This is the right starting point; it is not sufficient.

**The assignment before building:** Find 10 people with dense calendars who care
about their body. Run a manual version for one week: text them a gap you see on
their calendar + one exercise prescription. Measure: do they do it? Do they ask
for more? That's demand.

---

## Status Quo

Self-directed habits: people set their own calendar blocks, use Apple Watch stand
reminders, follow YouTube stretch routines, or use Pomodoro timers. The existing
cohort has already solved the motivation problem their own way.

Real competitor is not Wakeout or Stand Up! — it's the user's existing self-discipline
rig. Anyone who would immediately understand this product has probably already built
a worse version of it themselves.

---

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** Knowledge worker, 6–10 meetings/day, cares about their body but
loses the daily decision-cost battle. Motivated but frictionless-path-dependent.
The founder is the archetype.

**Narrowest wedge:** The full loop is the minimum viable product. The notification
without the prescription is useless (user said this directly: "they would not know
what to do"). The prescription without the calendar timing is just Wakeout. Both
halves are required. The wedge is: calendar gap detection + time-calibrated exercise
prescription, delivered as a single notification. Everything else (energy tap, evening
mode, posture timer) is layered on top.

**Free tier wedge:** Limited notifications + calendar sync. Proves the nudge is
useful before asking for money. Pro unlocks full exercise library + unlimited
notifications.

---

## Core Differentiator

**Prospective, not retrospective.**

Every competitor fires AFTER you've been sitting too long (Wakeout: Apple Health
detects stillness; Apple Watch: 50 minutes elapsed). This product fires BEFORE
the gap — it sees the opening in your calendar before you do and has a prescription
ready. The interaction model shift: reactive break → prepared recovery.

Wakeout could add calendar reads in one engineer's week. The moat is not the
calendar integration itself — it's the interaction model built around prospective
scheduling, and the first-mover habit loop.

---

## Constraints

- iOS first (EventKit: no OAuth, no API keys, one permission prompt — correct call)
- Google Calendar for Android/Workspace users: OAuth2, v1
- No server-side calendar processing — all gap detection runs on-device (privacy)
- Static exercise cards for v0 — no AI generation, no personalization engine yet
- 15 exercises covering desk, standing, breathing — sufficient for v0 content depth

---

## Premises

1. The user lacks decision-free, timed prescriptions that fit their actual schedule —
   not motivation.
2. The calendar gap is the unlock: prospective awareness beats retrospective nudging.
3. Wakeout owns mindshare in the content layer. Differentiation is scheduling
   intelligence, not the exercise library.
4. iOS-first (EventKit) is the correct v0 scope. Android is v1.
5. Free tier (limited notifs + calendar sync) reduces first-value friction.
   Pro at $7.99/mo is the upgrade once the habit is established. Churn is the
   real test — not conversion.

---

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Full v0 as specced (Calendar + Gap Detection + Exercise Cards)
Build the complete loop: EventKit integration, gap detection, time-calibrated
exercise prescription cards, posture timer, energy tap, evening mode.

Pros: Complete value proposition from day one. No "half-product" perception.
Cons: 2–3 months to ship. Demand unvalidated before build starts.

### Approach B: Calendar notification MVP only (2-week ship)
Ship ONLY gap detection + a single notification type ("You have N minutes before
your next meeting"). No exercise cards. Measure open rates and user behavior.

Pros: Ship in days. Validates the calendar-awareness hypothesis before building
the content layer.
Cons: User said directly — notification without prescription is incomplete. May
not retain.

### Recommended: Approach A, preceded by a 1-week manual validation sprint

Do the manual version first (text 10 people their gaps + prescriptions). If
3+ people engage daily and at least 1 says "I'd pay for this," build Approach A.
The manual sprint is 1 week. It de-risks 2 months of build.

---

## Open Questions

1. **Demand validation:** Do non-founder users respond to calendar-aware exercise
   prescriptions with the same intensity? Unknown.
2. **Churn:** Will users maintain the habit at month 2 and month 6, or does novelty
   drive early retention and real behavior determines the rest?
3. **Android timing:** EventKit advantage makes iOS the clear v0. When does
   Android demand justify the OAuth complexity?
4. **Content depth:** 15 exercises — enough for v0. At what point does content
   fatigue set in and what does that require (AI generation, user library, UGC)?
5. **Wakeout risk:** How quickly can they add a "check calendar before firing"
   toggle? That's their most obvious counter-move.

---

## Success Criteria

- Week 1: 10 manual participants, 3+ use prescriptions daily
- Month 1 post-launch: 30-day retention >40% (habit indicator)
- Month 3: Paid conversion from free tier >8%
- Month 6: Monthly churn <5% (indicates habit stickiness, not novelty)

---

## Distribution Plan

- iOS: App Store (primary)
- Android: v1 via Google Play
- Discovery: App Store search ("desk stretch," "office workout," "meeting break")
- Organic: calendar-aware notification screenshot is inherently shareable
- No paid acquisition until retention metrics are validated

---

## Dependencies

- EventKit (iOS native) — no external dependency
- Google Calendar API — OAuth2 setup for Android v1
- Illustrated exercise cards — design asset dependency before ship
- App Store review — 1–3 day buffer in launch timeline

Don't open Xcode yet.

manually, every day, for 5 days.

That's worth more than 2 months of build. Do it this week.
